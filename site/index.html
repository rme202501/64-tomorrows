What if your weather app was wrong?

A weather app shows you one number. Google's WeatherNext 3 AI model makes 64 forecasts for the same hours, and they do not agree. The spread between them is information your app leaves out: how sure the forecast is, and how that changes with the time of day, the weather, how far ahead you look, and where you are.

Act 2 · Rising insights Uncertainty grows, and not evenly

Two days ahead, how sure is the temperature?

The line is the most likely temperature. The shaded band is where 8 in 10 of the forecasts land, and the darker inner band holds the middle half. The band is the part of the forecast that a single line hides.

How fast does the gap grow, and where?

The gap between the 1-in-10 high and 1-in-10 low forecast, for four places and three variables. It starts narrow and can stay flat for a day, then jump: in Boston the temperature gap goes from about 4 °C to 13 °C between Tue 13 morning and afternoon, as the median moves with it. It depends on the place and the variable, so there is no single "how far can I trust it" number.

Where is the forecast foggier?

Temperature map with a fog layer: the more the 64 forecasts disagree, the thicker the fog. Press play to look a week ahead and watch the fog move with the weather.

Do forecasts jump, or drift?

Pick a moment. Each bar is what an earlier forecast run predicted for it, oldest on the left. The forecast for the same moment can jump from one run to the next as weather systems come through, then settle as the moment gets closer. Click a bar to map how that run differed from the newest one.

Act 3 · Main message Your weather app is lying to you

Your app picks one future. What does "40% chance" mean?

Each spin picks one possible future out of the forecast's range and maps it. The app only shows you one of them as the truth. Spin many times, and the share of spins with rain (or warmth) settles on the forecast's probability.

Simplification: each spin uses the same percentile everywhere on the map, so a spin shows a consistently warm (or wet) future, not a real single forecast run.

A yes-or-no question: will there be ice this week?

Chance of any precipitation, and of precipitation while it is below freezing (snow, sleet, freezing rain). Hatched spans show when ice would likely still be on the ground. A single "freezing rain" icon hides how unsure that answer is.

Does the spread change what you would do?

Wind chill and humidity combined with temperature. Pick an activity to see where and when conditions are good, and click a town for its hour-by-hour outlook.

Short-range forecasts are usually reliable, so the spread rarely changes a plan for tomorrow. It matters most for long-range plans and for extreme days, when the 64 forecasts disagree the most.

Act 4 · Solution Keep using your app, and learn when to trust it

  • Days 1 to 2. The forecasts agree closely. Trust the number.
  • Days 3 to 5. Check the spread before a plan that depends on a specific hour.
  • Beyond that. Read the spread as the forecast. A wide spread means plan for a range.

How many days ahead can I trust the forecast here?

Each place gets a "trust horizon": the first time its forecasts spread wider than the limit you choose. Lines mark 1, 2, 3 ... days. In this run, coastal towns have a slightly narrower spread than inland towns through day five.
