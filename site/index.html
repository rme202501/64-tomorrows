A weather app shows you one number. Google's WeatherNext 3 AI model makes 64 forecasts for the same hours, and they do not agree. Scroll on and the chart changes with the story: what the spread hides, when it matters, and how to tell when to trust a forecast.

Uncertainty grows, and not evenly

Step 1 · Two days out

For Boston, the gap between the 1-in-10 high and the 1-in-10 low is about °F at +24 hours. The forecasts mostly agree.

Step 4 · Then it jumps

Between +144 and +150 hours, Boston's gap goes from °F to °F. It is a step, not a slope, and a single line cannot show it.

Step 5 · It depends on what

Wind and rain follow their own patterns. Switch to wind and the gaps are different in every place, so there is no single "how sure is the forecast" number.

Forecast roulette: what does "40% chance" really mean?

Each spin picks one possible future out of the forecast's range and maps it. Spin many times, and the share of spins with rain (or warmth) settles on the forecast's probability.

Simplification: each spin uses the same percentile everywhere on the map, so a spin shows a consistently warm (or wet) future, not a real single forecast run.
